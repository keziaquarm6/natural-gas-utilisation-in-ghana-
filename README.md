Natural Gas Utilisation in Ghana: Development Trends, Prospects and Challenges

Author: Kezia Quarm Degree: B.Tech Petroleum Engineering (First Class Honours) Institution: Takoradi Technical University, Ghana Completed: August 2024 Supervisor: Mr. JJ Owusu Email: keziaquarm6@gmail.com LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/kezia-quarm-2b3489396

Abstract

Although still relatively young, Ghana's natural gas sector carries substantial untapped potential that, if properly harnessed, could drive the country's next phase of sustainable economic growth. As a clean, cost-effective, and environmentally sound energy source, natural gas is increasingly favoured by energy users over conventional alternatives. This study examines the development trends, prospects, and challenges associated with natural gas utilization in Ghana, drawing on secondary data from academic papers, journal articles, and published annual reports issued by the Ghana National Gas Company, the Ghana National Petroleum Corporation, and the Energy Commission of Ghana.

Findings indicate that natural gas offers considerable promise across multiple sectors of the Ghanaian economy — as an industrial feedstock, a transportation fuel, and a source for power generation. Domestic production has followed a steady upward trajectory, while gas imports from Nigeria have become progressively less dependable. The study identifies key constraints facing the sector, including a shortage of gas gathering and distribution infrastructure, weaknesses in fiscal and regulatory policy, limited storage capacity, and insufficient private-sector investment.
